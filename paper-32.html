Kilambi Research Working Paper No. KR-WP-2026-32

Relative Value Beyond the Pair Trade: Higher-Order Pricing Relationships, Their Enforcement Mechanisms, and Their Measurement

Kilambi Research

October 2026

Download PDF

Abstract

Relative value is usually studied as a property of a pair of prices. This paper studies it as a property of the mechanism that enforces the relationship between prices. We catalogue sixteen higher-order pricing relationships across rates, credit, repo, securitized products and market plumbing, and we classify each one by its enforcement mechanism: accounting identity, contract and statute, physical conversion, no-arbitrage subject to a balance-sheet band, and statistical regularity. For each relationship we derive the restriction, name the measurable variables, and report what a newly assembled record of public and regulatory data can measure. Three results stand out. First, clearing netting ratios are slow-moving state variables, not constants: over January 2001 to September 2026 the settled share of compared volume at the Government Securities Division averages 35.0 percent (standard deviation 4.7 percentage points), while the share of Mortgage-Backed Securities Division clearing par that settles on the Electronic Pool Notification system fell from 19.2 percent in 2001 to 2.6 percent in September 2026. Second, swap spreads implied by the September 2026 swap tape are negative at every tenor we can measure (medians of daily spreads: 2-year -13 bp, 10-year -39 bp, 30-year -70 bp), and a calibrated balance-sheet calculation puts the break-even 10-year spread near -30 bp, so the measured spreads sit inside the band that balance-sheet costs predict. Third, the largest apparent bases in our data are composition artefacts: a naively computed index-versus-basket gap for CDX North America Investment Grade averages -33 bp (standard deviation 14 bp) over September 2026 and contains no arbitrage. We close with three pre-specified tests for the highest-ranked relationships, each with a stated falsifier, and a list of relationships the public record cannot yet measure. Two of those tests have since been run on their stated terms, and both claims are withdrawn: the loan-tranche-ratings triangle (the collateral block added 0.0092 AUC on its holdout, below the pre-specified 0.01 threshold) and the balance-sheet factor test for the swap spread and the cross-currency basis. The third test, on the netting ratio as a state variable for settlement stress, proved not executable as designed, because the fails data its falsifier requires do not exist for its evaluation window; its claim is neither supported nor withdrawn.
