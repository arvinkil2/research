Kilambi Research Working Paper No. KR-WP-2026-33

The Price Is in the Protection: What 102 Sovereign Loan Files Show About Pricing, Security and Disclosure in Policy-Bank Lending

Kilambi Research

October 2026

Download PDF

Abstract

This paper assembles and codes the public record of 102 sovereign and sovereign-backed infrastructure loans across 34 borrower countries, with committed amounts of USD 240.1 billion, and asks three questions. What does a loan cost once every component of its price is counted? What does the lender take to protect itself, and what does that protection reveal? And what can an outside reader know about either, given what each side publishes? Three findings stand out. First, the headline interest rate is the smallest part of the price that can be measured: insurance premia running to 11.6 percent of facility value (a premium figure is held for only 6 of 102 deals), fees, trapped cash balances of up to USD 500 million on a single facility, and revenue capture all sit on top of it, and the premium is the rarest number in the archive (6 of 102, all six on Chinese facilities, none published by the insurer). Second, protection follows the lender, not the borrower: escrow or revenue capture is documented in 26 of 61 Chinese policy-bank deals and in none of 21 multilateral or DFI deals, the same sovereign receives a full revenue trap from one lender and an unsecured obligation from another, and in distress the cash traps that sat offshore kept working while no-comparability clauses did not survive a single systemic default and no asset pledge in the corpus is documented as enforced. Third, disclosure is the binding constraint: of 122 counterparties, 99 publish nothing at facility level, China Eximbank publishes no facility term for any of its 46 projects in the corpus, and the one window into a Chinese policy bank’s distressed book, a Hong Kong regulator’s template applied to China Development Bank’s branch, shows a defaulted Venezuela position carried at exactly 25.00 percent allowance cover for six consecutive reporting dates before cover rose to 81.2 percent. A per-deal data-depth audit (102 deals by 13 dimensions) scopes every claim in the paper: depth follows documents, and documents follow law, leaks and distress, never deal size.
