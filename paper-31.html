Kilambi Research Working Paper No. KR-WP-2026-31

The Iranian Rial as an Investment: Valuation, Relief Scenarios, and the Executability of an Offshore Position

Kilambi Research

October 2026

Download PDF

Abstract

This paper evaluates a specific trade: a foreign retail investor buys Iranian rials (IRR) at the October 2026 Tehran free-market rate of about 2.69 million per US dollar and holds them against the possibility of sanctions relief. The pitch rests on two true facts. The rial trades at about 1.2 percent of its JCPOA-era dollar value and at roughly one tenth of its purchasing-power-parity rate. We ask three questions in order. Is the currency undervalued? What does relief do to currencies like this one, on the record? And can an offshore holder acquire, hold, and above all exit the position? The answers are: yes, conditionally; far less than the pitch assumes, because in all seven unification episodes in the comparison set the exchange-rate gap closed by official-rate devaluation and in none by market-rate appreciation; and no, not at any documented price, because the documented Western bid for the rial is USD 0.00 and the lawful export quantity of notes from Iran is IRR 500,000, worth about USD 0.19. A five-scenario model built from the precedent record gives a best single outcome of 1.175x (partial relief, one year, frictionless) and probability-weighted theoretical returns of -7.8, -10.6, -37.6, -57.9, and -83.5 percent at six months, one, three, five, and ten years. Under the documented exit bid, the realisable recovery on a USD 10,000 stake at one year is USD 0.07 to USD 0.22. No legal instrument available to this investor pays off if and only if the rial appreciates.
