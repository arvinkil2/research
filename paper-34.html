Kilambi Research Working Paper No. KR-WP-2026-34

How Much Leverage Is Right? Growth, Ruin, and Volatility Across Asset Classes

Kilambi Research

October 2026

Download PDF

Abstract

One identical levered machine is run over the longest free daily histories of twenty return series: constant leverage, daily rebalancing, financing at the Treasury bill rate plus a class spread, and liquidation the first time a close breaches a stated maintenance covenant. The growth-optimal leverage, the multiple that maximises long-run growth under a stationary block bootstrap, is a small multiple everywhere: 2.5 times for the US equity market, against a Gaussian Kelly fraction of 2.64 computed on the same moments; 3 times for gold; 1.75 times for the reconstructed 10-year Treasury; 1 to 1.75 times for the single-name panel; 1 to 1.25 times for the commodity rows; and 1.25 times for bitcoin. The simulated optimum is at or below the Gaussian fraction in fifteen of the twenty rows. The exceptions are the rows whose Kelly fraction is below 1, where the optimum sits at the grid floor of 1 time, and one liquidation artefact, the yen at its admissibility cap. The survival-optimal leverage, the most leverage that keeps the five-year probability of liquidation or ruin at or below 1 percent and the probability of a 50 percent drawdown at or below 5 percent, is lower, and for most rows it is below 1 time. At 1 time the drawdown condition alone binds: the unlevered five-year probability of a halving is 10 percent for Coca-Cola, 42 percent for silver, and 87 percent for bitcoin. The pre-committed unifying test fails for both optima: optimal leverage times volatility is not constant across classes (coefficient of variation 0.87 for the growth optimum and 1.09 for the survival optimum), so the cross-class answer is not a volatility rule alone; the tails, the drawdown tolerance, and the maintenance covenant each carry part of it. Every ruin probability reported is a lower bound, because margin is checked at daily closes and never intraday.
