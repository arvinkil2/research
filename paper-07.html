Working Paper 07

Finite-term IP rights as an institutional investment thesis: evidence, pricing, and the central test

September 2026

Abstract

This paper tests whether a scalable institutional investment thesis exists around acquiring or financing finite-term (5–10 year) economic and commercialization rights in music IP, and whether the economics generalize to other royalty-producing assets. The thesis, as stated: underwrite 8–12% asset-level cash yields plus 5–10 percentage points of active commercialization uplift to earn 18–25% gross IRRs, without viral outcomes, aggressive leverage, or speculative terminal values. The paper assembles the evidence first: 50 sourced transactions, listed-fund realized returns, royalty decay and survivorship data, documented operating alpha, comparable structures, cross-asset evidence, legal feasibility across six jurisdictions, and failure cases. A deterministic cash-flow model (245 scenarios) then runs the central test. The conclusion is a single outcome of a three-outcome design: the evidence does not support the stated thesis, and the narrower disciplined-pricing variant does not survive either. The thesis is rejected.
