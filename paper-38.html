Kilambi Research Working Paper No. KR-WP-2026-38

Ontario's Global Adjustment: Anatomy, Facility-Level Evidence, and the 2027–2035 Outlook

Kilambi Research

November 2026

Download PDF

Abstract

Ontario's Global Adjustment (GA) is the charge that settles the difference between what the province's contracted and regulated generators are owed and what the wholesale market pays them. This paper builds the charge from its sources: a monthly actual series for January 2005 to September 2026 assembled from the Independent Electricity System Operator's own publications, a contract register of 3,582 facilities, and a facility-level reconstruction of the market revenue each generator earned, priced hour by hour at the market price (the Hourly Ontario Energy Price to April 2025, delivery-point nodal prices after the 2025 market renewal). Three findings follow. First, GA is a closed three-pool system whose published components sum to the total (in every month from 2011, and within C$1.2 million in the early workbook years), and its size is set by the contract book rather than by current market conditions: over the ten years to September 2026 it collected C$94.48 billion against C$47.11 billion of wholesale commodity value, C$2.01 of GA for every C$1 of energy. Second, the facility record prices 99.03 percent of 2024 generation but contract totals are public for only part of the fleet, so the reconstruction explains 43.5 percent of 2024 GA (C$3.67 billion of C$8.43 billion); the unexplained balance is named, and its largest block, Bruce Power's unitemised top-up, is bounded at C$1.53 billion to C$2.04 billion for 2024. Third, a forecast built pool by pool for 2027 to 2035 gives Total GA of C$6.79 billion to C$11.58 billion in 2027 and C$5.38 billion to C$12.70 billion in 2035 across the scenario grid. Under current Ontario Power Generation regulated economics the total declines in every cell; under the EB-2025-0297 settlement path it rises to a 2031 peak, because settled payment amounts grow faster than the contract portfolio decays. A backtest of the same machinery over 2020 to 2024 errs by -3.1 percent in the one year with no policy change and by +23.5 percent cumulatively, with the 2021 decision to move most non-hydro renewable contract costs to the tax base accounting for most of the miss. The backtest result is the paper's central caution: GA forecasts fail on perimeter decisions, not on prices.
